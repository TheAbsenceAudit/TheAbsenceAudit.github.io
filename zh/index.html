Why you can trust the numbers

Seven gates. Then three that exist only to void the other seven.

A concept enters the ledger only if it clears all seven: proven in the literature; absent from the market; capable of large unit margins; startable on little capital; quick to produce; addressing a market big enough to matter; and measurably better than the product the buyer already pays for — on that buyer's own metric, at the incumbent's own price, by a step change of at least two-fold. Cheaper, greener or biodegradable do not count. Those are preferences, not performance.

Then the economics are recomputed from scratch, in code, from cited inputs — feedstock cost, conversion yield, itemised equipment, the incumbent's price. Payback, margin and return are stress-tested against price falling, yield falling and capital doubling. And every surviving claim is re-checked by an independent audit pass that overrules our own scoring model when they disagree. It has done so 8 times.

“The Absence Audit” is the name of this method — an audit of what is absent: processes proven in the literature and missing from the market. The full method, and the record behind it, are published at/method/and/track-record/.
