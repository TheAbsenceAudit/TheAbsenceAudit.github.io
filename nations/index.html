The method, at national scale

Tunisia, audited.

The Absence Audit finds what is missing — and what the cited literature proves can fill it. Usually the unit is a process, a material, a venture. This time the unit is a nation: five structural absences, five literature-backed fills, and the closed loop that turns each liability into the next solution. It is not for sale. It is published free, in the open, as a template — for Tunisia, and for every country that carries the same absences. And because the first rule of this site is try to destroy the idea, the audit is then turned on itself: a red-team pass with honest confidence grades, a bottom-up cost model, and a kill-gate protocol — because the method is what transfers, not the dream. The two futures

Darwinian evolution — with the loop, and without it

The selection machine is already running. The loop is the intervention that flips which strategy wins.

Without the loop — the default

Dose accumulates: fluoride in the teeth before age 8, cadmium in the kidneys for decades, dust in the lungs. The fittest emigrate — engineer to Paris, founder to London; InstaDeep’s $680M exit is the emblem. Every departure makes the basin harder for those who stay. The nation evolves into a smaller, older, sicker version of itself that exports its children and imports its food. That future requires no decision.

With the loop

We change the environment, not the genes: clean water → intact enamel and strong bones within one generation; protein → nourished brains; jobs → a reason to stay; exports → room to breathe. Same people, same geography — a different fitness landscape. The plan is evolution by other means, and the loop is the environment we terraform for our own people first.

    Metals plant (~$100M, ~$78M/yr), solar, coastal desal, inland brackish desal, health at basin scale, the AI dispatcher, ponds and greenhouses scaled.

  5. STEP 4 — BUILD THE ARTERIES (years 5–10)

    The municipal spine ($1.2–2.1B class), the compute colocation park, PG-REE Phase B when the economics move, hydrogen, green belts, brine valorization at scale.

  6. STEP 5 — THE FULL LOOP (years 10+)

    The complete system at the original scale — the ~$21B vision, each leg having paid for itself. The loop is the destination; the steps are the proof.

The audit, turned on itself

Three independent deep-research passes over the techno-economic literature (materials, water/energy, compute/agriculture/financing, ~200 sources, citations resolved) and every figure recomputed in code from primitives (nations_arithmetic.py) feed the snapshot below.

Where the falsification bit

Seven findings — including two corrections to the arithmetic

Finding 01 — the arithmetic had a hole, and it was pumping The page's water transport figure is off by a factor of five. The report (and its arithmetic section here) states 0.38 kWh/m³ for 300 km + 400 m of lift. Lifting water is pure physics: 1 m³ x 400 m = 3.92 MJ = 1.09 kWh/m³ at 100% efficiency — before friction, pumps or motors. At 75% combined efficiency and real-wall friction the figure is 2.05 kWh/m³, i.e. pumping becomes 1.03 TWh/yr, not 0.19. Corrected total: 5.65 TWh/yr (5.32 in the report), and the solar park needs 3.23 GW, not 3.03. The error does not change the shape of the idea; it does change the credibility of the arithmetic — so we now publish both.

Evidence: Rw4 · Rw5; recomputation in nations_arithmetic.py

Finding 02 — the yttrium headline is the wrong headline 73.8% yttrium recovery is nearly economically meaningless. Yttrium trades at $5-10/kg. The Gafsa basket is Cerium-dominant (Ce 80-309 mg/kg of a ~322 mg/kg total), with La, Nd and Y after it, and negligible Dy/Tb. Magnet-grade NdPr is maybe 15-25% of the mass. At real prices the whole 2 Mt/yr circuit yields ~$5-10M/yr of contained metal (recomputed: 422 t REE at lab recovery; 7.0 M$/yr at a 45% industrial recovery and a 20% NdPr share) — against a chemical plant that moves 20 million m³/yr of acidic liquor.

Evidence: Rm1 · Rm3 · Rm8 · Rm9 · Rm15 · Rw1 · Rw5

Finding 03 — the acid mathematics nobody put on the ledger The published lab recipe, scaled, demands a chemical plant, not a refinery. S/L 1:10 at 1.65 mol/L: 601 kg of HCl-equivalent per tonne of phosphogypsum. For 2 Mt/yr that is 1,203,180 t/yr of HCl-equivalent — 3,759,938 t/yr of 32% commercial acid, at $ 564M/yr at EU spot prices, vs ~$7M/yr of metal revenue. Acid regeneration is the make-or-break engineering problem of the whole materials pillar — and no commercial plant doing it on sedimentary PG exists. The way out is to not re-digest the PG at all — recover uranium + rare earths from the phosphoric-acid stream instead (Unlock 01).

Evidence: Rm4 · Rm16 · Rm10 · Rm12

Finding 04 — the radioactivity question was not asked Gabès PG sits at 214-970 Bq/kg of Ra-226; the IAEA/EU exemption line is 1,000 Bq/kg. This is not a containment afterthought: HCl leaching carries Ra into solution, Ra co-precipitates with the REE product (a poisoned, unsellable concentrate), or you drop it back into the residue with scavengers — leaving 2 Mt/yr of still-radioactive filter cake. NORM disposal runs $15-420 per 55-gallon drum. The "clean gypsum" claim in the loop is a laboratory idealization; at industrial scale the NORM problem is the project, not a footnote.

Evidence: Rm11 · Rm12 · Rm13 · Rm5

Finding 05 — the water is municipal-scale — except for one crop Conventional agriculture cannot pay €1.10-1.40/m³; the desert truffle can. Delivered desalinated water inland costs €1.10-1.40/m³ (Jubail 3A's record €0.38/m³ at the plant gate, plus $1.5-2.1B of pipeline and ~1.5 kWh/m³ of pumping) — 30-40x what Tunisian irrigation water costs today (€0.02-0.05/m³). Wheat, olives and dates are structurally unable to carry that, and the Great Man-Made River ($33B) and Riyadh's transfers exist because cities, not fields, can be cross-subsidised. But the truffle is the exception: 200-250 mm/yr = 2,000-2,500 m³/ha, × €1.30 = €2,600-3,250/ha/yr of water, against €7,500-22,500/ha gross — water is 11.5-43.2% of revenue. Exactly one crop in this loop monetises expensive desalinated water in an open desert field, and it is the truffle. (Brackish groundwater cannot substitute: the Helianthemum host tolerates salt, but Terfezia claveryi establishes only in near-fresh soils of ~0.8 dS/m.)

Evidence: Rw3 · Rw5 · Rw7 · Rw8 · Rc15 · Rc7

Finding 06 — the compute pillar is a diplomacy problem, not an engineering one 300 MW of leading-edge accelerators cannot legally be bought today. 2024-26 US BIS rules: the UAE received 35,000 GPUs under a strict "Compute Diplomacy" agreement and was elevated to Country Group A:5 in July 2026; Saudi Arabia remains case-by-case with a 35,000-unit cap. Tunisia has no such bilateral AI-security agreement. ~300,000 accelerators (300 MW) is a different order of magnitude entirely, and the "sovereign AI in the mines" premise therefore depends on a diplomatic negotiation between Washington and Tunis — not on geothermal gradients, workers or transmission lines. European alternatives exist but are capacity-constrained; Chinese hardware ends the story of western-aligned software ecosystems.

Evidence: Rc5 · Rc6 · Rc4

Finding 07 — the truffle never needed the panels' land (correction) Decoupled from the solar array, the truffle's binding limits are the market and the inoculum — not light, not land. An earlier version of this audit flagged an agronomic conflict between the full-sun Helianthemum host and agrivoltaic shade. That conflict was an artefact of a needless assumption: the loop never required the truffle to grow beneath the panels. Planted as full-sun orchards on Tunisia's vast arid state land (~86,000 ha earmarked for 40-year leases under Law 93-120 [Rc19]), land is simply not a binding factor. The real ceilings are two. First, the market: the global desert-truffle trade is a ~100 t/yr wild-harvest niche, and cultivated supply beyond ~1,000-1,500 t/yr breaks the €20-60/kg band [Rc18] — capping the program at ~2,600-4,000 ha. Second, inoculum: ~6,000 mycorrhized seedlings per hectare means a 2,000 ha orchard needs 12 million of them [Rc16], and the 150 ha Saudi precedent largely failed [Rc17]. This finding supersedes the earlier "agronomic incompatibility" claim.

How it could actually run

Kill-gate protocol — the template transfers, the gates stay

The honest answer to "what happens when one link fails" is: the loop was the wrong unit of build. The right unit is the module with its own gate, financed on its own cash flow, and a public go/no-go criterion. This is the graveyard logic of the ledger, applied to a nation: the plan includes its own falsification.

    Fifty million dollars of pilots, not twenty billion of faith. 100 ha of inoculated Helianthemum with three seasons of yield data; a 50 t/yr acid circuit with a published mass balance (the acid-recycle number is the gate); a radon + geotechnical survey of the actual Gafsa workings; one 10 MW datacenter pilot with a signed anchor tenant and a published PUE; a 100k m³/day coastal RO unit operating in the STEG framework.

  2. P1 — First bankable modules (years 1-4)

    Three 300-450k m³/day desalination plants for municipal and industrial offtake (EIB/AfDB/Global Gateway financing; the 17 GW renewable programme is the natural umbrella); ~700 MW of solar via the proven IPP route; 1,000-2,000 ha of truffle orchards on full-sun sites (without shade panels — Finding 07); a 20-50 t/yr REE partnership with a research university and an EU CRMA offtake conversation.

  3. P2 — Scale the winners (years 4-8)

    Only modules that cleared their gates: 500 Mm³/yr if urban/industrial offtake is signed (coastal distribution, not the 300 km agricultural loop); 2.5-3.5 GW of PV if the grid programme and PPAs support it; truffle exports at scale if the 3-season record holds; compute only if a hardware path and a PPA exist.

  4. P3 — The loop (years 8-15, optional)

    If-and-only-if every module is cash-positive alone. The loop is a design principle for sequencing, never a justification for any single project. This is the same discipline the ledger applies to ventures: the graveyard is part of the method.

What the red team could not kill

Water, re-scoped: Tunisia's dams and aquifers are genuinely exhausted, and coastal desalination at municipal scale is standard, proven and fundable (€0.38-0.60/m³ at the plant gate, mega-plants at $800-1,200 per m³/day, LCOW record €0.38/m³). What dies is the 300 km agricultural delivery — no country on earth won the subsidy arithmetic on that.

Solar, at the right pace: the IPP framework works (500 MW at ~$0.031/kWh); the target should be the grid programme's pace, not a 3 GW island.

Desert truffle, decoupled and honestly capped: the one module where commercial cultivation exists (Spain, since 1999), yields average ~376 kg/ha over 20 years, water is small (200-250 mm), protein is real (14-22.5% dry weight [Rc8]), farm-gate is €20-60/kg [Rc9] — and the one crop whose margin absorbs desalinated water (11.5-43.2% of revenue [Rc15]). Land is not the limit: ~86,000 ha of leasable arid state domain [Rc19]. The real limits are the luxury market (~1,000-1,500 t/yr → ~2,600-4,000 ha before the €20-60 band breaks [Rc18]) and inoculum supply (~6,000 mycorrhized seedlings/ha [Rc16]; the 150 ha Saudi precedent largely failed [Rc17]). A 2,000-3,000 ha program is a genuine €15-45M/yr export — real, but bounded, not a desert-transforming industry.

Strategic metals, re-specified: the PG re-leach is dead, but the metals are not. Recover uranium + heavy rare earths from the phosphoric-acid stream that GCT already runs — a two-decade-proven flowsheet (Freeport, 14M lbs U3O8) now being rebuilt at ~$100M by OCP/Morocco [Ru1, Ru2]. At Gafsa scale that is ~$78M/yr of clean strategic-metal revenue (uranium carries it; the heavy REEs ride along), with the remaining PG REE left as a future option. That is the honest fill for the "strategic materials" absence — gated on a Tunisian pilot, like every other unlock.

Compute, at national scale, of any kind: the deepest absence of all. Finland, Sweden, Iceland made the move; Tunisia has the talent (InstaDeep's $680M exit is the proof) — the blockers are hardware law and grid, not intelligence. Re-scoped, this is a commercial data-economy play on a benign-site, anchor-tenant model.

The unlocks

Every wall, and what opens it

A red team that only kills reads as self-sabotage. The method's second half is the same one applied to a dead-end venture: re-specify the constraint, then re-derive from the terrain. Most of what the audit called impossible dissolves when you reason from what Tunisia actually is — a coastal, sun-drenched, phosphate-rich, EU-adjacent country — instead of from what the first-order plan assumed. Each unlock below is tagged terrain (first-principles reasoning from the country's actual geography, resources and institutions) or research (a cited industrial precedent), and each carries the thing that would prove it.

Unlock 01 — the materials pillar was leaching the wrong thing Recover uranium and rare earths from the phosphoric-acid stream, not from re-digesting the phosphogypsum. The red team was right that re-leaching the PG is dead — but the PG was never the resource. During wet-process acid production, 85-90% of the uranium and 15-40% of the rare earths dissolve into the phosphoric acid, while >80-90% of the radium stays behind in the gypsum solid [Rm11] [Ru1]. Recover the metals there and three walls vanish at once — the acid wall (the acid circuit already exists at GCT's Gabès plant), the radioactivity wall (Ra never co-precipitates with the product, because it never left the gypsum), and the grade wall (uranium, not Cerium, carries the value). The route is proven, not hypothetical. Uranium recovery from wet-process phosphoric acid ran commercially in the US for two decades — Freeport (Louisiana) produced 14 million lbs U3O8 over 1978-99 at >95% recovery via DEHPA/TOPO solvent extraction, and phosphate-derived uranium supplied ~20% of US uranium in the 1980s [Ru1]. It is being revived in 2025: OCP's URANEXT is building Morocco's first yellowcake plant for ~$100M at El Jadida [Ru2], and SX operating cost is $59-83/lb against an $80-95/lb spot price [Ru3]. Applied to Gafsa's ~6.5 Mt rock/yr at ~50 ppm U [Ru5]: ~325 t U3O8/yr (≈$61M/yr) plus ~585 t/yr of recovered REE oxide (≈$18M/yr), for ~$78M/yr of strategic-metal revenue from a ~$100M plant — against the dead PG re-leach's $5-10M on a $125-560M acid bill. The residual gypsum still holds ~65% of the REE as a future option, but the uranium and the heavy-REE fraction come out clean.

Evidence: Ru1 · Ru2 · Ru3 · Ru4 · Rm11. The ladder still applies: this is an L2/L3 unlock (proven precedent + published economics), gated on a Tunisian pilot — not an L4 committed project.

Honest boundaries

What this is — and what it is not

It is

A concept-level engineering study plus its own published falsification test: seven layers of claims graded by evidence confidence, a bottom-up CAPEX/OPEX/revenue model recomputed in code from sourced primitives, downside cases, financing instruments that actually exist, and a kill-gate protocol with observable go/no-go criteria. Free, public, indexable — a template, not a product. The whole point of the red team is the method it demonstrates: a country's idea survives only as exactly as honestly as a venture's.

It is not

A commissioned state program, a bank-grade (Class-5) feasibility study, an investment offer, or a claim that any of this has been built. Nothing here is advice to any government. A confidence grade measures the evidence, not the probability of success: several layers are graded HIGH precisely because the research shows the difficulty is real. The quantitative reasoning is grounded in cited literature; nothing on this page replaces geological, radiological, geotechnical or EPC-level surveys. Parts of the report remain deliberately exploratory — simulation-driven “cognitive travel” replacing physical space programs, for instance. Verify every figure and citation before relying on any of it — the same rule as everything else on this site.
