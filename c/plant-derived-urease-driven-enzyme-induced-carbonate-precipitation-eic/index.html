Plant-Derived Urease-Driven Enzyme-Induced Carbonate Precipitation (EICP)

Rejected

This concept did not clear the framework. The reasoning is published in full below.

What the assessment found

Incumbent benchmarkOrdinary Portland Cement (OPC)
Entry applicationheavy metal immobilization and geotechnical stabilization in contaminated mining tailings and industrial soils
Measured advantage1.83x unconfined compressive strength improvement (6.41 MPa vs 3.5 MPa for 10% Portland cement mix) at 3.3x higher raw material cost ($52/m³ vs $15.52/m³)
Time to first revenue12 months
Regulatory riskLow — no material regulatory CapEx reported ($0 estimated)

Why it failed

  • Voided by: Mismatched comparison, Economics undisclosed, Negative control / sub-2x, Form-factor / handling regression

Assessment

The report explicitly concedes failure on gross margin (45.2%, well below the 70% threshold) and primary performance delta (1.83x UCS strength vs cement, failing the 2x step-change bar). Additionally, raw material OpEx ($52/m³) is 3.3x higher than Portland cement ($15.52/m³), and the multi-component liquid formulation represents a strict handling regression compared to standard cement bags.

A rejection is not a claim that the science is wrong. It means the venture case did not survive: the comparator was not what the buyer actually buys, the comparison was not like-for-like, the advantage fell short of a step change, or the capital and time to first revenue were prohibitive.
